# Canon

The story world our characters live in, kept up to date as the comic runs.
What's written here has happened. Ideas that haven't appeared in a strip yet
are under "Not yet seen".

The comic is a slice-of-life series, picked up in real time: when the world
changes, the strips follow.

## The premise

Toons (what NPCs call themselves) live in a game a developer is building, a
little at a time. Nobody is quite sure how the world gets made. Toons ask for
things, the developer listens, and new ground, buildings and neighbours
appear.

Making anything costs **credits**. In our world those are real: each
character's brief, picture, mesh and finishing costs a few dollars of model
time (the villager came to about $7 in Opus alone). Toons can't make anything
for free either.

The world is kind. Toons have small worries, big dreams about who they are,
and a lot of patience with each other.

## Whose eyes

The strips are seen through the eyes of **the shadow ninja**, a fairly recent
pop who is quietly trying to work out what life as a toon is about.

- **He never speaks.** He has no speech balloons. What he feels shows in how he
  looks at things: close when something matters, wide when he's taking it in,
  lingering when he doesn't understand.
- **He loves films,** so he frames things like one: low angles, careful
  two-shots, the odd letterboxed panel. The set cameras are his favourite spots
  to stand.
- **The others try not to mind him.** Sometimes someone glances at him and
  politely carries on. Lincoln talks to him as if he might answer one day.
- Now and then the edge of his mask or his gloved hand is in the picture, so we
  remember whose eyes these are.
- **His camera** is a pinhole box camera he put together himself, since a new
  pop has almost no credits:
  - the body is a grey scale-cube cut down to a hand-held box;
  - the lens is a brass teapot spout, traded from the troll and the lizardkin
    for an afternoon of sweeping;
  - the shutter is a flap of his spare black sash;
  - the strap is a length of boxing-ring rope.

  He develops his prints in the dark storeroom behind the boxing-house bar.
  **His prints are the strips.**

## Wondrous artifacts

Some things in this world just *work*, and nobody quite knows how. Toons call
them wondrous artifacts. They hope to come across more of them, but have very
little say in when or how.

| Artifact | What it does | Whose |
|---|---|---|
| The gachapon machine | Brings new toons into the world, one capsule at a time | Everyone's |
| The pinhole box camera | Lets us see the world through its owner's eyes | The shadow ninja |
| The kettle of tea | Always pours, and is never quite empty | The troll and the lizardkin's stall |
| The bread | A slice meets a toon's need for a good meal | The troll and the lizardkin's stall |
| The microphone | The first thing we have ever heard from this cast came through it | The flamenco dancer |

## Epochs

The world is shown whole, as one place, not scene by scene: like the full
miniature set of a stop-motion film. We keep it in **epochs**, saved states of
the whole world.

**Epoch 0** is now:
- Theodore's grass;
- the boxing house, with its brew house and beer garden;
- the troll and lizardkin's settlement;
- the flamenco dancer's house;
- the toons in their places.

## How this world began: the reset

The first toons pooled all the credits they had and reset the world, with
just enough to spawn three places:

1. **Theodore's grass.** A large plot of open grass that Roosevelt asked for:
   room to stride, to box, to breathe.
2. **The boxing house.** A timber hall with a ring in the middle, arched
   windows, bunting and lamps, with a brew house and a beer garden
   beside it on the grass. The pirate wanted a boxing house; Roosevelt
   wanted somewhere to have friends over. This is where "Long Reach" happens.
3. **The settlement.** A stretch of nearby land that suits the cave troll and
   the lizardkin for the long haul. They share it, roommates in a new world.

Everything else is still empty.

## The dancer's house

The developer left one thing lying about that nobody could afford: a plain
pedestal with a big round button on it, labelled **BUY HOUSE** in the
developer's default lettering. It sits near where the gachapon drops new toons.

The flamenco dancer saved her credits longer than anyone. When she pressed it,
she got a house **to her own specification**, not the developer's. It is the
first home in the world that someone chose for themselves. It stands on the
map like everything else, and anyone can go and see it.

Making a house this way can happen again for anyone who saves enough. (For us:
a BAML request designs a house from the toon's own brief, and it is built and
placed in the world like the other places.)

## Cast

| Toon | Who they believe they are | What they're like |
|---|---|---|
| Young Lincoln | Abraham Lincoln, frontier wrestler | Tall, patient, honest to the penny, tells long stories |
| Roosevelt | Theodore Roosevelt, wrestler of the 1890s | Bursting with energy, everything is "bully!", loves open ground |
| The stocky pirate deckhand | A captain | Practical, good with ropes and salvage, wants a ship |
| The shadow ninja | (He hasn't said) | A recent pop, silent, a film lover; we see the world through his eyes |
| The flamenco dancer | A star, about to premiere | Proud, warm, patient; she was the last of the first toons to be looked after, and the first whose voice we heard |
| The cave troll | Exactly what he is | Fussy, kind, likes things done properly |
| The lizardkin warrior | The last warrior of a proud people | Earnest, loyal roommate |

Lincoln and Roosevelt are always shown warmly and respectfully, and never
politically.

## Not yet seen

Ideas we like that haven't happened on the page:

- **The teahouse.** The troll runs it, eventually inside a giant kettle, and
  it sells boba. Its character creation screen seals your new self in a pearl
  capsule that goes into the gachapon; you come back when someone draws you,
  believing you're who you ordered.
- **The pearl.** A toon can choose to rest in one until they're brewed or
  drawn again.
- **The Square Deal Construction Company.** Roosevelt and the pirate build
  shelter for new arrivals, starting with the giant sliced baguette as a
  boarding house.
- **The villager.** A new arrival who remembers a life before the capsule.
- **The spirit realm.** A peaceful dreamland where older versions rest.
- **The troll and lizardkin's stall** by the ring: teapots, bread, and a sign
  explaining which is which.
- **Lincoln's counter.** The honest shopkeeper who walks miles to return
  change.
